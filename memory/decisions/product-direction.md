# Product direction: today, coming, receipts

Status: active on `shelfscale-open`. A proposed successor for the site's framing, [data-first.md](data-first.md), is on branch `shelfscale-data-first` for Elliot's review; this file stays in force until he adopts it.

Decision date: 2026-11-06

Decision maker: Elliot Karikari.

Decision:
1. On every screen, ShelfScale answers three questions for each product sold: is it less healthy under the rules today, will it still be when the rules change, and can every number be proved? Anything that serves none of the three does not belong.
2. Bulk scoring is a first-class surface. A food business's problem is its whole range.
3. ShelfScale scores products as sold, never diets or food as eaten. Packaged products score as the unit on the shelf; loose items at their evidence-based typical weight with its real range. Recipes stay in scope because for a small producer the recipe is the product.
4. The user is the small, independent food producer checking their own products, often for a first retail listing. Features whose natural home is a technical team at a large manufacturer are out of scope.

Copy rules that follow: plain statement of what the product does; no naming or comparison with other tools; no em dashes; no invented numbers; estimated or caller-supplied figures labelled; public copy never names the project's academic, employer or retail origins. Every score shown or exported carries its law (model, version, status) and the data release.

Evidence: Elliot's brief of 2026-11-06.
